# Sculpture and Painting production content pilot

Source comparison performed on 2026-10-13 by Codex. The reviewer metadata names
Codex explicitly; no museum endorsement or independent human editorial approval
is claimed. Human editorial review remains a release gate. The content is sourced
from collection publications rather than copied from legacy files or synthetic
fixtures. Source URLs, locators, original/version identities, dating precision,
reviewer and review time are stored with the records/evidence.

## Registered teaching content

There are 18 original works and 54 teaching records, plus four technique-definition
records: 58 active records, 70 evidence records. No separate review records exist.
Each artwork is taught in recognition p1, creator p2 and dating p4, with the same
stable artwork identity. Four works start in A, three in B and two in C in each
level. Concept IDs are new stable pilot IDs; no existing concept/image IDs change.

### Terminology: Sculpture l5 p1 only

Carving, modelling/modeling, casting and assembling/assemblage have four concise
source-based definitions, grounded in the [National Gallery of Art technique
guide](https://www.nga.gov/educational-resources/explore-basics-sculpture), with
[Getty's four-technique explanation](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/classroom_resources/curricula/sculpture/background2.html)
as a cross-check. Six explicit, reviewed meaning contrasts supply twelve directed
negative assertions. Adding a different term ID does not automatically certify
new negatives. These are contrasts between defining operations; a sculpture can
use several or all of the processes. Forward definitions are options-only, so
valid paraphrases are not rejected by exact typed recall. All four start in A;
this is a representative terminology slice, not a completed Basics level. Tool
p2 and material p4 remain empty. Its p3/p5 diagnostic pools can reuse p1 only.

## Assessment scope and proof

- Recognition refers to the particular original object described by its collection
  entry. The complete identity set identifies that referent; it does not enumerate
  every sculpture or painting with a similar motif. Clues include documented
  distinctive details and version/location context, not the full answer title.
- Creator questions explicitly assess the collection's principal artist credit.
  A complete set concerns that named attribution field, never every workshop
  participant or every historical attribution. No generic claim that different
  artists cannot collaborate is made. Both Michelangelo works remain valid in
  reverse questions.
- Dates use `dateInterpretation: "catalogue-label"`, with matching structured
  `dateBasis` qualifiers. Questions and statements explicitly say that the
  collection entry **lists** a complete dating label. This avoids converting
  overlapping, approximate or alternative scholarly dates into globally false
  statements. It is collection-dating recall, not a universal proof of an exact
  creation year. Approximate dates have no invented numeric tolerance bounds.
- Dating values have canonical IDs independent of artwork/curriculum IDs. Creator
  typed blanks accept explicit reviewed names/aliases with case/space
  normalization. Recognition titles and dates are options-only in this pilot.
- Optional reverse creator assessments have independently reviewed scope wording.
  Reverse date and definition assessments are not enabled.
- Candidate pools remain in curriculum scope. The shared semantic context supplies
  truth and evidence, not extra off-curriculum answer choices.

## Research exclusions and corrections

The Doni Tondo was not used because its Uffizi date header and explanatory text
conflict. Giambologna's preparatory clay model was not confused with the later
marble group; conflicting model dates also led to exclusion. The Last Supper
page's narrative starts in 1494 while its date table starts in 1495; both agree on
1498 as the end, so only completion is assessed. Mantegna uses the current Brera
entry's c.1483 rather than an older c.1480 exhibition text. Raphael's dating is
explicitly the fresco-cycle interval, not an invented wall-specific exact year.
Perseus uses the original bronze's completion date, not the shop reproduction.
The Cantoria subject omits its creator's name, avoiding answer leakage; the
Sacrestia delle Messe qualifier distinguishes Donatello's different gallery.

## Images

No image asset or binding has been added or marked approved. All 18 recognition
records use independently source-checked authored text through the existing
assessment stimulus mechanism, including matching. No images are downloaded,
hotlinked or generated. Image identity, reproduction rights, hosted derivatives,
bytes/checksums and final alt-text/credit leakage review remain pending.

The [Uffizi's image-use policy](https://www.uffizi.it/en/professional-services/publications)
provides a permission process, not blanket approval for the application. Florence
Cathedral's work pages expressly credit photographs to Antonio Quattrone and the
Opera. Public availability of those pages is not treated as a reusable image
license. Future image approval can replace a text stimulus without changing fact
IDs; independently approved text remains the fallback.

All 600 requested quizzes contain eight exercises with exact A/B/C quotas and
one safe four-pair matching exercise. No validation failures or duplicate answer
cards were found. There are 700 True and 700 False statements. Terminology adds
60/60 ready requests; creator inverses add 120/120. Generated prompts/options and
representative complete quizzes were inspected in addition to checking counts.

The wider 24,000-request Art sweep produces 1,380 complete quizzes and 22,620
explicit shortages, with zero validity/composition violations. The extra ready
pools include cumulative Level 10 review of the pilot facts, not new Level 10
content or a complete Art curriculum. Existing archived difficulty-review items
remain diagnostic review items, not active content validation errors.

Full automated suite: 13 suites, 426 tests passed. After the final source-scope
wording refinement, the affected pilot/upgrade suites were rerun: 210 tests passed.
TypeScript and lint were rerun and passed without warnings.
Pilot-specific regressions cover stage restrictions, exact quotas, deterministic
coverage, real one-to-many credits, accepted typed forms, source withdrawal,
source-qualified uncertain dates, review reuse, distinct matching cards and
immediate-lock compatibility. Simulator checks have not been performed.

Sequential admission is unchanged. Painting l2 and Sculpture l3 remain locked in
a fresh player profile until earlier levels are completed. This pilot does not
fabricate prerequisite facts, change unlock rules or reset unrelated progress.
A development simulator profile must have the relevant prerequisites completed
before UI inspection. Engine generation readiness is not player access readiness.

The process is reusable for other curated original works and carefully reviewed
terminology. It does not establish automatic negative certification for open
genres, interpretations, movements or arbitrary paraphrases, and it does not
qualify the other 140 target relations or all remaining Art levels.
