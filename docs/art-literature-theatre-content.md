## Factual qualifications preserved

- Original author/playwright/poet credits distinguish translation, editing,
  fictional narration and later adaptations. Negative byline comparisons assess
  a documented original credit; they do not close an author's whole oeuvre.
- Genre examples and representative writers have visible, named-work or
  named-writer constraints. A different book's documented byline can contradict
  that constraint without asserting exclusive genre membership.
- First publication differs from composition, first serialization, first book
  edition and later revision. Huckleberry Finn uses the British 1884 debut;
  Treasure Island uses its 1881 serial debut, with the 1883 book distinguished.
  The Maltese Falcon's 1930 book is separated from its 1929 serial.
- Wordsworth's first three-stanza printing and Dickinson's initial edited
  “The Chariot” text remain qualified. Horace Smith's other Ozymandias sonnet is
  not confused with Shelley's; the author is not leaked in the poet question.
- Birth countries use present-day borders. Grass's Danzig birth is mapped to
  Poland, Stoppard's Zlín birth to Czechia, and Camus's Mondovi birth to Algeria;
  nationality is a separate field.
- Hamlet's approximate composition and The Bacchae's posthumous performance
  dating are not turned into secure exact years. Tartuffe's five-act version,
  Doctor Faustus's surviving printed edition and modern-play premieres are
  visibly scoped to the correct event or version.
- Theatre venue opening dates refer to the named building phase: Salle
  Richelieu's 1790 opening differs from the company's 1799 arrival; Ford's 1863
  rebuilt theatre differs from the 1862 Athenaeum and 1968 restoration.
- Epic formation ranges, oral traditions, textual versions and later copies
  stay distinct. Shahnama and Popol Vuh are included as epic/mythological works,
  without claiming their assessed textual versions were composed in antiquity.
- Character-family questions assess a specified singular relationship revealed
  early in the text; they do not assert a complete set of relatives or companions.
  The Yellow Wall-Paper narrator is not assigned the disputed name “Jane”.
