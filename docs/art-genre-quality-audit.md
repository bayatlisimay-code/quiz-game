## How to read the decisions

“Keep” means the current answer is a suitable teaching primary, not the work's only legitimate classification. “Change — editorial” means a better primary is recommended, not that the current answer becomes false. “Change — wording/refinement” preserves the underlying classification or chooses its established parent/subtype. “Provisional” marks a specific evidence or editorial limitation, not implementation approval.

The **recommended primary column is editorial judgement**, informed by the cited sources and the user's approved answers. Direct authorial labels, scholarly interpretations, institutional explanatory accounts and catalogue attestations are distinguished in each row. A catalogue subject tag establishes that a label has been applied; it does not establish educational primacy or an exhaustive set of genres. Automatically generated Gutenberg summaries were not used as evidence. Publisher prose, scholarly editions and author statements carry more weight than retailer/subject tags.

The alternatives column is deliberately **non-exhaustive**. Plain entries have positive support in the cited sources or the original text; entries explicitly marked as qualified readings, form, style or context are not silently promoted into new unconditional genre assertions. An omitted label is **unknown here, never false**. None of these tables certifies incorrect alternatives or supplies negative evidence for quizzes.

There are several different classification axes:

| Axis | Examples | Consequence for teaching |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story/dramatic genre | Gothic fiction, adventure fiction, tragedy, comedy | Suitable primary answers where recognisable and well supported. Parent and subtype may both be true. |
| Literary/dramatic form | Epistolary novel, memory play, linked stories, verse drama, musical | Forms are legitimate learning objectives, but should be identified as forms rather than silently treated as exclusive narrative genres. Musical is explicitly approved for Oklahoma!. |
| Movement, style or tradition | Realism, Romantic drama, expressionism, Theatre of the Absurd, epic theatre | Established theatre classifications can be useful primaries if their historical sense is clear. They can overlap story genres. |
| Mode or technique | Satire, parody, allegory, metatheatre, non-linear narration | A named genre such as satirical fiction can be justified by a sustained mode; one instance of the technique does not classify every work. |
| Subject, setting, audience or theme | War, whaling, Yorkshire, childhood audience, gender, class | Not interchangeable with genre. War fiction and Western are recognised genres, but a war reference or western location alone is insufficient. |

“Love story”, historic literary “romance”, Shakespearean romance and modern commercial romance are not interchangeable. Genre membership is non-exclusive; a primary answer must never turn another supported description into a negative alternative. Repeated labels such as Gothic fiction are an editorially acceptable outcome of this audit, even if they make the existing matching cohort infeasible. No primary recommendation was chosen to manufacture matching coverage.

## Specific issues requiring an editorial decision before repairs resume

1. **Apply the already approved primaries in a later implementation only.** This report records approval; it does not change content. Nineteen Eighty-Four and Oklahoma! are not silent additions.
2. **Prefer educational recognition over answer-label diversity.** Recommending Gothic for both Wuthering Heights and Dracula, or Adventure for several novels, may require a later reserve/replacement proposal for matching. Do not change priorities or pick an obscure true classification just to obtain four distinct labels.
3. **Don Quixote needs its qualification retained.** Picaresque is a documented affiliation, not an uncontested all-purpose definition of the whole work. Satirical fiction is the recommended broad learning focus; parody does not make chivalric romance false or true in every scope.
4. **Our Town and Top Girls remain flagged.** No sufficiently compelling narrower primary has been established here for Our Town. Top Girls has strong feminist/political scholarship, but a theme-to-genre leap would repeat the problem being audited. Both current answers remain provisionally retained pending a concrete primary decision.
5. **Master Harold replacement evidence needs strengthening.** Realist drama is the provisional editorial recommendation; the retrieved primary archive and education source do not by themselves supply the ideal direct critical genre locator. Do not treat this row as implementation-ready evidence.
6. **Cyrano and Six Characters recommendations cross classification axes.** Romantic drama is a broad editorial umbrella in the former; metatheatrical drama foregrounds form in the latter. Their current labels are not false. The form/style versus genre scope needs approval before any semantic rewrite.
7. **Keep credible minority interpretations as qualified alternatives.** The Pride and Prejudice dissertation and Frankenstein student essay are evidence of particular Bildungsroman readings, not proof that this is the preferred teaching genre. University hosting is not equivalent to a settled scholarly consensus.
8. **Chekhov needs authorial/critical attribution.** Comedy and tragicomic readings must coexist. Neither a tragic ending nor a director's treatment overrides Chekhov's own terminology.
9. **None of the rows certifies genre negatives or completeness.** A later shortage repair must still verify every displayed incorrect alternative and every matching edge under the actual wording. A primary-genre decision alone cannot supply those negatives.
